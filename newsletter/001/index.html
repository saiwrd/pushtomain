Beyond the SaaS bubble

AI is rewriting go-to-market. Almost nobody is writing about it from outside SaaS. That blank map is the opportunity.

Something real is happening in AI and go-to-market. Not the demo-reel stuff — the part where a small team can stand up signal engines, propensity models, and outbound rails that used to take a RevOps department and a six-month vendor cycle.

The writing about it is thin. Outside of a few operators who actually ship — Jordan Crawford being the clearest voice — most of what you get is tool tours, prompt paste-bins, and “AI SDR” product launches. Useful if you sell seats to other software companies. Less useful if you sell anything else.

SaaS is where the templates live. Clay recipes. ICP frameworks tuned to ARR bands and employee counts. Sequences that assume your buyer already lives in LinkedIn and reads fifteen cold emails a day. That world is crowded. The playbooks are shared. The inboxes are burned.

Meanwhile there is a whole economy that does not fit those templates: mineral rights, clinics, industrial hardware, robots on trucks, M&A theses that do not show up in a SaaS TAM slide. The buyers are real. The signals are public — filings, job boards, exclusion lists, capital events — they just are not packaged for a growth team that only knows ARR.

That is the work I do. Find who is worth a conversation the week it matters. Build the system that keeps finding them. Put a human on the phone when the system surfaces something worth saying. Ship the rails so it does not collapse when I am not in the room.

This letter is for CEOs in that blank part of the map. What I am building. What is converting. What is noise. When I have something worth your time, you will get it here.

If that is you: subscribe, or email me and tell me what you sell.

— Joel
