# Cold email is the smallest part — Push to Main · Issue 01

> Revenue leaders are hungry for GTM engineers. What they want from one is bigger than sending email.

Live: https://pushtomain.xyz/newsletter/001/

Big things are happening for AI in go-to-market. The clearest way to see it is to listen to the people who own the number.

A friend just hosted more than a hundred CROs and VPs of Sales over a couple of days, from companies you'd recognize. Outbound and cold calling came up, as they always do. The conversations that stuck were about AI in GTM and what a GTM engineer is actually for.

His read: these leaders are hungry for GTM engineers, *as long as* you're not just slinging email. They want the top-of-funnel work, meaning lists, messaging, call prep, and personalization. More than that, they want TAM modeling, segmentation, experiments, and validation of their own hypotheses so they can move faster and smarter.

One enterprise VP of Sales with a seventy-person team is using a GTM engineer to show her CRO, sales ops, and marketing what is possible with targeting and segmentation. It isn't a tool purchase. It's a demonstration.

## That's the job

Most of the talk about GTM engineering is still about sequences and enrichment tables. That part is crowded, especially in SaaS, where everyone runs the same playbook against the same inboxes. The bigger opportunity is upstream, in questions like these:

- Where should we play, and how big is it really?
- Which accounts are in-market this month, and why?
- Is this segment real, or is it a slide?
- Should we hire for this vertical, or test it first?

In practice, that looks like a VP who has waited twelve weeks for a list from sales ops getting it in a few days, scored, with the reasoning attached. It looks like marketing anchoring an ABM strategy in real accounts before the quarter is gone. It looks like a CEO finding out whether a new market is worth a hire before making the hire.

None of that is cold email. All of it is go-to-market.

## Where the leverage goes

The GTM engineers who win the next few years will sit with revenue leaders in both sales and marketing. They'll speak that language, clear the blockers that drive leaders crazy, and show what good data and signals can do for steering a large revenue org. That earns trust, and trust turns into budget.

Outside a handful of operators who actually ship, with Jordan Crawford the clearest voice among them, there isn't much written about this from inside the work. That's why I'm writing this letter.
