22 Things We'd Do In 30 Days If The Product Already Works (It Does)

Orchestry has 400+ customers, a horizontal market that looks like confetti, and a distribution problem. Month one is conversations. The pie chart can wait in the lobby.

A note from Joel, up front I didn’t think you’d actually read a full 30-day plan. So I made it a little more entertaining. Same plan. Fewer slides. Meaner to TAM. Kind to people who pick up the phone.
JM
Joel Martinez Posted on September 15, 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  Unofficial BuzzFeed energy

1. Admit this is a distribution problem.

You already won the “does anyone want this” round. Congratulations. The rest of the category is slowly noticing Copilot turned governance from a nice-to-have into “please do not let the intern paste the board deck into ChatGPT.” It’s a race now.

PMF is not hiding in a spreadsheetvia every strategy offsite since 2019

Impact: we stop workshopping positioning like the market is theoretical.

2. Get pipeline from people saying hello with their mouths.

If you need meetings in 30 days, you are cold calling. Email is a teammate. Ads are a teammate. Neither of them is a throat.

HELLO?the sound of a calendar becoming a business

Impact: the calendar starts looking like a company that sells things on purpose.

3. Sort the list like a bouncer.

Phone-intent first. High-intent numbers go to AEs. Low-intent goes to email the same day. No ceremony. No “we’ll circle back to these 4,000 rows.”

Not on the list, palvoicemail, removed from the VIP rope

Impact: AEs stop speed-dating voicemail.

4. Worship connect rate, not “activity.”

A short list that answers beats a thousand heroic dials into the void. You do not need to cosplay a 2014 boiler room.

4–6% is not a personalityit’s a list problem

Impact: inbound AEs can do this without becoming hunters.

5. Have a backup for “we don’t really cold call.”

60–90 minutes a day from a third-party dialer, or the new outbound AE who actually wanted this job. Nobody has to win a culture debate first.

Plan B has a headsetand a timer

Impact: conversations still happen.

6. Call your own customers like a journalist, not a closer.

Why they bought. Why they stay. Why the last tool became expensive shelf art. This is market development, not SDR theater.

“So… why did you stay?”the only discovery call that matters

Impact: every email, ad, and talk track is their language.

7. Say the quiet part: most governance is unusable.

Other tools fail because the structure is not usable. This is context operations paired with governance — data you can actually act on, not another report that goes to die in a Teams tab named “FY25.”

Pretty dashboard. Zero decisions.shelf-ware’s official portrait

Impact: a reason to talk that isn’t “we also do Microsoft.”

8. Check whether the email machine is actually a machine.

Audit week one. Add domains and inboxes until tens of thousands of sends a month is boring. Never from the primary corporate domain, unless you enjoy explaining to IT why the CEO’s mail is in spam.

Inbox 14 is not a vibeit’s capacity

Impact: the offer gets a fair fight, and we can actually cover the slivers.

9. Three experiments a week. A thousand sends each. Minimum.

A/B the line. Spintax the words, not the soul. Zero positives at 1,000 → kill it. A pulse → take it to 3,000. Opinions get less voting rights than replies.

1,000 or it didn’t happensorry to the draft in Notion

Impact: we know what the market said, not what we liked in Google Docs.

10. Email one vertical at a time, like a person with boundaries.

The pie is many tiny slivers. Good. We go deeper in one, then another. “Horizontal” should not mean “spray and pray but make it a slide.”

Concrete this week. Law next.not all 40 at once, you maniacs

Impact: depth inside micro-markets without boiling the ocean.

11. Run two situations, not forty industries.

Copilot they can’t turn on without leaking. The vertical where the logos already exist. Day 30 is a kill/double, not a TED Talk about TAM.

A / B / park the restthe whole strategy on a sticky note

Impact: a decision instead of a museum of industries.

12. Ask the 400 who they know.

Mine the graph. Ask on the customer calls. The warmest pipeline in the building has been sitting in the CRM the whole time, waiting to be invited.

“Anyone else drowning in sprawl?”referrals: still undefeated

Impact: highest-quality pipeline in the shortest time.

13. Steal the event badge scan, ethically and with spreadsheets.

Attendees of the Microsoft / SharePoint / Copilot / partner events that matter → same bouncer rules. Call or email. A list with a timestamp and a reason.

Hello from booth 12lanyards, but make it outbound

Impact: we stop pretending another TAM dump is a list.

14. Lurk the competitors in public.

LinkedIn listeners on the people already shouting in this category. Anyone who engages is a lead, not a vibe.

Liked, commented, employedoutbound’s love language

Impact: humans who already raised a hand, just not at us yet.

15. Go where they already complain.

Reddit, Discord, Slack, X. One foothold each. Listen. Quote. Do not arrive with a webinar.

r/sysadmin at 2amdistribution you don’t have to rent

Impact: we’re in the rooms ads keep trying to buy.

16. Leave the ads on.

Do not pause them to “think.” Do not pour more money in until the loop works. Inbound is tired, not dead. We rejigger. We do not hold a funeral.

Pause is not a strategyit’s a nap

Impact: the engine you already paid for keeps running while we fix the line.

17. Make ads that look like the buyer, not the product’s headshot.

Statics and UGC. Many variants from one brief. Kill duds in about 48 hours. 80% of money on things that already worked. Creative finds the IT person. Twelve interest stacks do not.

This IT admin walked so your CTR could runlook like them. sound like them.

Impact: the algo gets a face to find. Targeting theater gets a day off.

18. If an ad wins, it gets its own house.

Own campaign. Own page. Headline matches the ad. If Google is involved, it matches the keyword too. Shocking, we know.

H1 = the promise they clickednot the product name, you cowards

Impact: the click and the landing page are in the same conversation.

19. Google only for people already in trouble.

Bottom-funnel phrases. Keyword family = ad set. Cut junk. Cut terms you already rank for (that’s just tithing to Alphabet). Optimize for booked meetings, not “what is SharePoint” at $12 a click.

guest access reviewsearch intent: please send help

Impact: we stop paying for education and for traffic we already own.

20. Stop introducing yourself to people you already met.

Same emails we outbound sit in Customer Match. Pixel the site. About 10% of budget follows anyone who showed up once. Remarketing, but with dignity.

We have got to stop meeting like thispixel says you were here Tuesday

Impact: we stop paying twice to find the same person.

21. One screen that tells the truth.

Ads + meetings + CRM in one view. Agents draft creative and flag corpses. Humans still push the spend button. We argue about numbers, not folklore.

Dashboard or it didn’t happenscreenshots are not a data warehouse

Impact: every kill/double is from a live view.

22. Every week, one page. Day 30, a decision.

Conversations. Quotes. What we kill. What we send more of. One ask. Then: which situation, which verticals, whether the phones stay on, whether ads keep / cut / concentrate. SDRs only after a line already gets conversations.

Month two gets a machinemonth one had a hypothesis. we prefer the machine.

Impact: they can run the company without reading a novel. (You’re welcome.)

Bring to the first week

Tool admin. Customer intros. Ads data. A yes/no on who dials this week. The “please don’t email the Department of Justice by accident” list. ACV so the math isn’t interpretive dance.

If a meeting slips, we still call. We still send. The pie chart can wait in the lobby.
